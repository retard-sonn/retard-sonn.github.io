Chapter One

Where the wire went

Other children asked what the computer could do. I wanted to know what was on the other side of the cable, and who was allowed to decide.

That question never really closed. A packet leaves this machine and crosses maybe fifteen strangers' hardware before it arrives, and every one of those hops is a place where somebody made a decision about trust. Most of my education since has been an argument with those decisions.

Chapter Two

Six years, one uniform

Sainik School Goalpara had my entire adolescence on a timetable, and I have never once been grateful for that in the moment.

Reveille before the light. Inspection. Prep. Lights out whether or not you were finished. You do not get to negotiate with a bell, and after six years the bell stops being outside you.

It is the least glamorous thing on this page and easily the most useful. Nearly everything I have shipped since was built in the hours nobody was watching — which is, I suppose, why I ended up building software about exactly that.

Chapter Three

The curve I'm on

I read Data Science & Artificial Intelligence at the Indian Institute of Technology, Guwahati. BSc, Honours. Still in progress — I am not going to pretend otherwise.

The honest version of a degree in this subject is that you spend the first stretch learning that almost everything interesting is an optimisation problem wearing a costume, and the rest learning how badly it goes when your data disagrees with your assumptions.

Guwahati is forty minutes from where I grew up. I did not go far. I did go deep.

Chapter Four

A tree that only grows if you told the truth

WatchMeGuru is mine. It is a student accountability platform for the people I grew up beside — JEE, NEET, UPSC, NDA, CBSE aspirants, all of them promising themselves things at eleven at night. I built its AI backend.

Every accountability app I had ever used ran on the honour system, which is a polite way of saying it ran on nothing. So this one does not take your word for it. You claim the work, then you photograph the work. A vision model reads the photograph and returns one of three verdicts. Your streak moves only on verified.

Easier to show you. Go on — try to grow it.

Chapter Five

Breaking things carefully

My niche is penetration testing and bug bounty. I want to be precise about where I actually am with it, because this is a field where people inflate and get found out in one interview.

I hold the Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate. That is the only certificate I hold, and I am not going to list a wishlist next to it and hope you do not check. What I have besides that is a lab, a lot of hours, and the specific stubbornness of somebody who grew up asking where the wire went.

The reason it suits me is the same reason the networking question never closed. Security is not really about tools. It is about noticing the assumption everybody stopped checking.
